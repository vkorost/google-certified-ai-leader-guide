# Glossary

Every term this book teaches, what it means for answering a question
about it, and where it is covered. The objective and the chapter follow
the term in brackets. Generated by `Guide/working/glossary.py`; do not
hand-edit.

**accountability** (4.3, chapter 22). Somebody being answerable for a decision. Impossible without explainability.

**agent** (1.3, chapter 12). Software pursuing a goal, forming a view of its situation, and acting on it with tools. Drop any part and it is something else.

**Agent Assist** (2.3, chapter 10). Real-time help for the live agent. It never speaks to the customer.

**Agent Platform** (2.4, chapter 11). The unified platform: models, build tooling, deployment, management.

**Agent Search** (2.3, chapter 10). Search and recommendation over your own content, and the prebuilt route to retrieval.

**Agent Studio** (2.5, chapter 13). The console workspace for prompts and agents. Where you build for production at scale.

**AI on the edge** (2.5, chapter 13). Running close to where the work happens, for latency or unreliable connectivity.

**AI Principles** (4.3, chapter 22). Google's published principles, which this material sits under.

**AI-first** (2.1, chapter 8). Google's claim of continuing investment and continuous updating. The answer to a fear of falling behind.

**anonymization** (4.3, chapter 22). Removing or obscuring personal detail so it no longer identifies anyone.

**Apps Script** (2.5, chapter 13). Low-code building inside Google Workspace.

**AppSheet** (2.5, chapter 13). No-code application building, for a function with no engineers.

**artificial intelligence** (1.1, chapter 2). Machines doing work that would otherwise need human intelligence. The outer ring: it does not imply learning from data.

**automatic model upgrade** (3.1, chapter 18). Managed models improving underneath you, so what you tested may not be what runs later.

**AutoML** (2.4, chapter 11). Training on your own data without machine learning expertise. Answers a skills gap, not a data gap.

**bias** (3.1, chapter 14). Skew learned from training data and sometimes amplified. The cause behind an unfair outcome.

**BigQuery** (2.5, chapter 13). Querying and analysing at scale. BigQuery ML trains models from SQL, where the data already sits.

**bottom-up** (4.1, chapter 20). Employees finding the practical applications and feeding back. The other half.

**business requirements** (4.1, chapter 19). What the business needs the solution to do. One of the two influences on gen AI needs.

**chain-of-thought** (3.2, chapter 15). Supplying the intermediate reasoning steps in the prompt.

**change management** (4.1, chapter 20). Carrying the organisation through adoption. A tool nobody uses has the impact of one that does not work.

**clear vision** (4.1, chapter 20). The starting point of a gen AI plan.

**cloud computing** (2.1, chapter 8). Consuming that capacity without owning the estate.

**Cloud Functions** (2.5, chapter 13). Runs a small piece of code when an event happens.

**Cloud Run** (2.5, chapter 13). Runs containers, serverless, scaling to zero when idle.

**Cloud Storage** (2.5, chapter 13). Where files and objects live: documents, images, audio, anything unstructured.

**Cloud Video Intelligence API** (2.5, chapter 13). Analysing video content, for search, recommendation and media analysis.

**Cloud Vision API** (2.5, chapter 13). Analysing image content: objects, text, faces, landmarks.

**code generation** (1.1, chapter 19). A solution shape: suggesting snippets, completing functions, writing tests.

**connectivity** (4.1, chapter 19). What network you can rely on. Poor connectivity points at the edge.

**Contact Center as a Service** (2.3, chapter 10). The whole cloud-native contact centre: telephony, routing, IVR, virtual agents, assistance.

**content moderation** (3.1, chapter 14). A human-in-the-loop position: judging user material in context.

**context window** (1.1, chapter 7). The amount of input a model can hold in view while answering. A fixed property of the model, not a setting.

**continuous improvement** (4.1, chapter 20). Refining solutions as feedback and data arrive.

**Conversational Agents** (2.3, chapter 10). The customer-facing virtual agent. Handles what it can and escalates to a person.

**Conversational Insights** (2.3, chapter 10). Analysis of conversations that already happened: topics, sentiment, trends. Past tense.

**create summarize discover automate** (1.1, chapter 2). Google's four-verb shorthand for what gen AI is for.

**custom agent** (2.4, chapter 11). An agent built for a defined job, wired into the systems it needs. Integration and rules, not model behaviour.

**Customer Engagement Suite** (2.3, chapter 10). The exam guide's name for Google's customer-experience products.

**customization** (4.1, chapter 7). How specialised this has to be, and whether behaviour can be shaped to your requirements.

**data accessibility** (1.2, chapter 4). Whether data is available, usable and in a consumable format. Owning it and reaching it are different.

**data analysis** (1.1, chapter 19). A solution shape: finding insight and relationships inside documents and data.

**data center** (2.1, chapter 8). The physical estate underneath the cloud.

**data dependency** (3.1, chapter 14). Performance resting on the training data, so flaws in it flow through. Systematic and upstream.

**data governance** (2.1, chapter 8). Control over who may use data and how, alongside security and privacy.

**data ingestion** (1.1, chapter 3). Collecting raw data and bringing it in. Ingestion moves data.

**data preparation** (1.1, chapter 3). Cleaning, correcting and reshaping it into usable form. Preparation changes data.

**data quality** (1.2, chapter 4). Whether data is accurate, complete, consistent and relevant. Relevance is the one you cannot see by inspection.

**data stores** (2.5, chapter 12). An agent tool category: a route to information the agent can look up.

**deep learning** (1.1, chapter 2). Machine learning using neural networks of many layers. It implies nothing about generating anything.

**deterministic agent** (2.5, chapter 12). Also called traditional. Paths and actions laid down in advance; same input, same route.

**diffusion model** (1.1, chapter 2). A model that produces content by refining random noise into structure. Imagen is the Google example.

**Document AI** (2.5, chapter 13). Extracting data from documents of varied formats, and automating the processing.

**Document Translation API** (2.5, chapter 13). The same, with the formatting preserved. The deciding word is shape, not language.

**drift monitoring** (3.1, chapter 18). Watching accuracy change over time with Model Monitoring. Drift produces no error.

**edge case** (3.1, chapter 14). A rare, atypical situation that exposes weakness. Reliable on the ordinary, erratic on the unusual.

**enterprise-ready** (2.1, chapter 8). Responsible, secure, private, reliable and scalable. The answer to a safety worry.

**ethical guidelines** (4.3, chapter 22). The organisational form of those principles, which a strategy establishes rather than assumes.

**explainability** (4.3, chapter 22). Being able to account for why a particular decision came out as it did.

**exploration** (4.1, chapter 20). Encouraging experiment and collaboration to find what is worth doing.

**extensions** (2.5, chapter 12). An agent tool category: a standard way into somebody else's service through its API.

**fairness** (3.1, chapter 14). Whether outcomes are equitable. A judgement about results, not a property of data.

**Feature Store** (3.1, chapter 18). Managing and serving the feature data a model consumes, one definition shared across models.

**few-shot** (3.2, chapter 15). Asking with several. The answer when output must match a shape or format consistently.

**fine-tuning** (1.1, chapter 7). Further training on your examples to improve behaviour on your tasks. It teaches behaviour, not facts.

**first-party data** (3.3, chapter 16). The organisation's own records and content. The cue is possession.

**foundation model** (1.1, chapter 2). A large model trained on vast unlabelled data, giving it breadth and making it adaptable to many tasks.

**functions** (2.5, chapter 12). An agent tool category: a named action running logic you wrote.

**Gemini** (1.4, chapter 6). Google's multimodal model: several data types, conversation, content creation, long-context reasoning.

**Gemini Advanced** (2.2, chapter 9). The same surface with more capability, enterprise-grade protections, and Gems.

**Gemini API** (2.5, chapter 13). How Gemini is reached programmatically, from either studio.

**Gemini app** (2.2, chapter 9). Google's chat surface: writing, summarising, translating, images.

**Gemini Enterprise** (2.2, chapter 9). Unified search and agents over scattered internal sources. The answer to silos.

**Gemini Enterprise for Customer Experience** (2.3, chapter 10). The study guide's name for the same area. Google's two documents disagree.

**Gemini for Google Cloud** (2.2, chapter 9). The assistant for code, cloud management, data and security posture.

**Gemini for Google Workspace** (2.2, chapter 9). Gen AI inside Gmail, Docs, Slides and Meet.

**Gemini Nano** (2.5, chapter 13). Google's most compact model, built to run on devices.

**Gemini Notebook** (2.2, chapter 9). A research assistant grounded in the files you upload, which points back to them.

**Gemma** (1.4, chapter 6). Open, lightweight and customisable, for local deployment and specialised work. Not a lesser Gemini.

**Gems** (2.2, chapter 9). Saved instructions making responses consistent, for work repeated in a fixed shape.

**gen AI landscape** (1.3, chapter 5). Google's five-layer picture: infrastructure, model, platform, agent, application.

**gen-AI-powered application** (1.3, chapter 5). The user-facing layer, where a person meets the capability.

**generative agent** (2.5, chapter 12). Behaviour specified in ordinary language and carried by an LLM.

**generative AI** (1.1, chapter 2). An application of machine learning whose output is new content: text, images, audio, video, code.

**Google AI Studio** (2.5, chapter 13). Free, for quick prototyping. Exploration rather than production.

**Google Cloud API Library** (2.5, chapter 13). The catalogue where the pre-built APIs are found.

**Google Search** (2.3, chapter 10). Search over the world. The contrast the blueprint draws against Agent Search.

**GPU** (2.1, chapter 8). The general-purpose accelerator alternative for the same workloads.

**grounding** (3.1, chapter 14). Tying output to sources somebody can verify. It changes where the answer comes from, not the model.

**Grounding with Google Search** (3.3, chapter 16). Grounding in the public web, in real time.

**hallucination** (3.1, chapter 14). Fluent, confident output based on nothing real. Answered by grounding.

**human in the loop** (3.1, chapter 14). A person inside the workflow, approving or reviewing. Five named positions, from moderation to post-release review.

**hybrid agent** (2.5, chapter 12). Both at once. The deterministic half holds what must not vary; the generative half absorbs what nobody could enumerate.

**hypercomputer** (2.1, chapter 8). A supercomputing system of performance-optimised hardware, open software and ML frameworks.

**Identity and Access Management** (4.2, chapter 21). IAM. Who may use or see a resource. It prevents access.

**image generation** (1.1, chapter 19). A solution shape: producing pictures from a description.

**Imagen** (1.4, chapter 6). Text to image, by diffusion. It makes pictures; it does not read them.

**infrastructure layer** (1.3, chapter 5). Compute and storage: servers and accelerators, plus the software to run and keep models.

**key performance indicator** (3.1, chapter 18). The operational number a function already tracks. How business impact gets measured.

**knowledge cutoff** (3.1, chapter 14). Training stopped at a date, so the model may know nothing after it. Answered by retrieval.

**labeled data** (1.2, chapter 4). Data carrying a tag that says what each example is. Usually produced by humans, one record at a time.

**large language model** (1.1, chapter 2). A foundation model specialised to human language. Every LLM is a foundation model; the reverse does not hold.

**latency** (4.1, chapter 19). How long one response takes. Not throughput, which is how many at once.

**LiteRT** (2.5, chapter 13). The Lite Runtime: tooling for deploying models onto edge devices.

**low-code** (2.1, chapter 8). Tooling that needs little code, so non-specialists can build.

**machine learning** (1.1, chapter 2). The part of AI where the rules are learned from data rather than written by a person.

**measure impact** (4.1, chapter 20). Showing the effect on business goals, in the function's own operational numbers.

**metaprompting** (3.2, chapter 15). Using prompting to produce, modify or interpret other prompts.

**ML lifecycle** (1.1, chapter 3). The path a model takes: ingestion, preparation, training, deployment, management.

**MLOps** (2.4, chapter 11). Tooling that lets teams collaborate to monitor and improve models in production.

**modality** (1.1, chapter 7). The data types a model takes in and gives back. The first thing to settle when choosing one.

**Model Builder** (2.4, chapter 11). Where a model of your own gets built with a machine learning framework.

**model deployment** (1.1, chapter 3). Making a trained model available to be used.

**Model Garden** (2.4, chapter 11). A catalogue for discovering, customising and deploying existing models. Not a registry of yours.

**model management** (1.1, chapter 3). Maintaining, watching and keeping a deployed model current.

**model training** (1.1, chapter 3). Building the model from prepared data.

**multimodal** (1.1, chapter 2). One model holding more than one data type, on the input side, the output side or both.

**multimodal search** (2.2, chapter 9). One search spanning several data types, rather than one search per format.

**Natural Language API** (2.5, chapter 13). Insight from unstructured text: sentiment, classification, entity extraction.

**natural language processing** (1.1, chapter 2). The field of getting machines to work with human language. A field, not a layer in the stack.

**neural network** (1.1, chapter 2). Interconnected nodes loosely modelled on a brain. An architecture, not a capability.

**no-code** (2.1, chapter 8). Tooling that needs none at all.

**one-shot** (3.2, chapter 15). Asking with a single example.

**open approach** (2.1, chapter 8). Choice across offerings, including open and third-party models. The answer to lock-in, specifically.

**output length** (3.3, chapter 17). A cap on how much is produced. The answer for too long, and for inconsistently long.

**performance tracking** (3.1, chapter 18). Reviewing the model's metrics to see how it is doing.

**personalized user experience** (1.1, chapter 19). A solution shape: adapting what is produced to one person over time.

**Pipelines** (3.1, chapter 18). Automating the machine learning steps, repeatably.

**platform layer** (1.3, chapter 5). API access to models, data management and deployment tooling, with the infrastructure hidden.

**plugins** (2.5, chapter 12). An agent tool category: extra skills and integrations added on.

**post-generation review** (3.1, chapter 14). A human-in-the-loop position: continuing feedback after release.

**pre-generation review** (3.1, chapter 14). A human-in-the-loop position: validating output before anybody relies on it.

**pre-trained model** (2.1, chapter 8). A model already trained, ready to prompt. Removes the building, not the data problem.

**prebuilt RAG** (2.4, chapter 11). Managed retrieval over your own content, delivered with Agent Search.

**prioritize use cases** (4.1, chapter 20). Deciding the order deliberately rather than accidentally.

**privacy** (4.3, chapter 22). Managing the risk carried by personal data.

**prompt chaining** (3.2, chapter 15). Carrying a conversation across turns so context accumulates.

**prompt engineering** (1.1, chapter 15). Building the input well, so the response suits the need. Changes what you send, not the model.

**prompt tuning** (1.1, chapter 15). A tuning technique, so it adjusts the model side rather than the message side.

**pseudonymization** (4.3, chapter 22). Replacing identifiers with substitutes, keeping the link back separately.

**RAG** (2.4, chapter 11). Retrieval-augmented generation. Retrieve, fold into the prompt, generate, optionally iterate.

**RAG APIs** (2.4, chapter 11). Retrieval components to compose into an application you are already building.

**ReAct** (3.2, chapter 15). Reason, then act. The prompting pattern underneath agent behaviour.

**reasoning loop** (2.5, chapter 12). An agent's repeating cycle: notice, interpret, plan, act, and again.

**reinforcement learning** (1.1, chapter 3). Learning by acting in an environment and keeping what earns reward rather than penalty.

**resourcing** (4.1, chapter 20). Investing in data strategy, using what exists, developing AI talent.

**responsible AI** (4.3, chapter 22). Making sure applications do not cause harm and are used ethically, across the whole lifecycle.

**reusing prompts** (3.2, chapter 15). Keeping the ones that work as templates instead of rewriting them.

**ROI** (4.1, chapter 20). What a programme is eventually judged on. Assembled from operational numbers rather than measured directly.

**role prompting** (3.2, chapter 15). Assigning a persona, which shifts style, tone and focus. The answer for consistent manner.

**safety settings** (3.3, chapter 17). Screening output for harmful or inappropriate material. They do not detect falsehood.

**sampling parameters** (3.3, chapter 17). Google's umbrella term for the five settings that shape output without changing the model.

**saved info** (3.2, chapter 15). Details stored in Gemini so the model holds them consistently without being told again.

**scale** (4.1, chapter 19). How many people will use it.

**secure AI** (4.2, chapter 21). Protecting the AI application from harm, specifically attack and misuse.

**Secure AI Framework** (4.2, chapter 21). SAIF. Helps organisations manage the risks of AI and ML models.

**secure-by-design** (4.2, chapter 21). The protections Google builds into network and hardware, rather than ones you configure.

**Security Command Center** (4.2, chapter 21). One view of security posture: misconfigurations, exposure, threats. It reveals problems.

**security patch** (3.1, chapter 18). The ordinary software obligation, which does not lapse because the software contains a model.

**Speech-to-Text** (2.5, chapter 13). Speech becomes text; audio and video get transcribed.

**strategic focus** (4.1, chapter 20). Prioritising a small number of implementations with clear business value.

**structured data** (1.2, chapter 4). Data with a predefined schema, easy to search. Rows, columns, agreed types.

**supervised learning** (1.1, chapter 3). Training on labelled examples to predict a known target, whether a category or a number.

**technical constraints** (4.1, chapter 19). What the environment will not allow. The other influence.

**temperature** (3.3, chapter 17). How adventurous word choices are. Lower it for focused, deterministic output.

**text generation** (1.1, chapter 19). A solution shape: producing written content.

**Text-to-Speech** (2.5, chapter 13). Text becomes natural-sounding speech, for voice interfaces.

**third-party data** (3.3, chapter 16). Data from another organisation, licensed or supplied.

**token count** (3.3, chapter 17). Text measured in meaningful chunks. Not the context window.

**top-down** (4.1, chapter 20). Leadership setting vision and strategy. Half of what Google recommends.

**top-p** (3.3, chapter 17). Nucleus sampling. Bounds the candidate pool by accumulated probability; the same job as temperature, differently.

**TPU** (2.1, chapter 8). Google's custom-designed processors, specialised for machine learning and strong at parallel work.

**Translation API** (2.5, chapter 13). Language conversion across text, documents, web pages, audio and video.

**transparency** (4.3, chapter 22). Being open about how a system is evaluated and what data it uses.

**unlabeled data** (1.2, chapter 4). Raw, untagged data. What foundation models are trained on at scale, so not the inferior kind.

**unstructured data** (1.2, chapter 4). Data with no predefined shape: prose, images, audio, video.

**unsupervised learning** (1.1, chapter 3). Finding groupings, patterns and anomalies in data with no target column. Segmentation is the standard case.

**user interaction** (4.1, chapter 19). How people will engage with it, which shapes the surface.

**Veo** (1.4, chapter 6). Video generation, from a text description or a still image.

**versioning** (3.1, chapter 18). Tracking versions of a model with Model Registry, so you can say what ran and when.

**video generation** (1.1, chapter 19). A solution shape: producing video from text or a still image.

**workload monitoring** (4.2, chapter 21). Watching whether running systems behave as they should.

**world data** (3.3, chapter 16). What is publicly known. The answer when nobody involved owns the fact.

**zero-shot** (3.2, chapter 15). Asking with no examples.
