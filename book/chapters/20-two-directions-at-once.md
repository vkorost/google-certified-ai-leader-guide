# Chapter 20: Two Directions at Once, and How You Prove It Worked

**Summary:** *Google's advice on gen AI strategy has a shape, and the shape is the examinable part: leadership sets direction and employees find the applications, both at the same time. An option offering only one of those halves is the most convincing wrong answer on this objective, because it is correct as far as it goes. The second half of this chapter is measurement, where the right answer is always the operational number the business already cares about.*

## One Half of a Correct Answer

A company is setting its gen AI strategy and wants to follow Google's recommendation.

The options will include a purely top-down approach, in which leadership owns the vision and hands it down. That is half of what Google recommends, stated accurately, and it is wrong.

Google's position is explicit: combine a **top-down** approach, where leadership sets the vision and the strategy, with a **bottom-up** approach, where employees identify the practical applications and feed back what they find.<sup>[2]</sup> The option that names both, however it is labelled, is the answer.

*However it is labelled* is doing real work in that sentence. Google's own documents name the two halves and never name the combination, so **a question needing a single word for it has to invent one**, and the word it usually reaches for is **multi-directional**. That option will look wrong on the page for exactly the reason it is right: the other three are quotations and it is not. The one that **is not a quotation** is the only one that is **not half an answer**.

**Two directions at once** is the idea, and it is worth understanding rather than memorising, because the reasoning transfers. Leadership knows what the business is for and cannot know where the friction actually is. The humans doing the work know exactly where the friction is and cannot authorise anything. Neither group can run this alone, and a strategy that comes from only one of them fails in a predictable direction: top-down alone produces initiatives nobody needed, and bottom-up alone produces experiments that never scale.

## The Six Things a Strategy Has To Carry

Google's own list, and each item answers a question somebody will ask you.<sup>[2]</sup>

| Element | What it commits to |
|---|---|
| **Strategic focus** | Prioritising a small number of implementations with clear business value |
| **Exploration** | Encouraging experiment and collaboration to find out what is worth doing |
| **Responsible AI** | Ethical guidelines, and development that is secure and responsible |
| **Resourcing** | Investing in data strategy, using what you have, developing AI talent |
| **Impact** | Measuring the effect on business goals, and showing the benefit is real |
| **Continuous improvement** | Refining the solutions as feedback and data arrive |

Notice that **strategic focus and exploration sit next to each other and pull against each other.** One says pick a few things with obvious value. The other says let people try things whose value is not yet obvious. Both are on the list, and holding both at once is the actual job rather than a contradiction to resolve.

Three of the six are tested by nothing in circulation: strategic focus, resourcing, and the clear-vision item below. They are exactly the sort of surface this exam can ask about without warning.

## And the Six Steps of Planning One

Separately, Google sets out what planning one involves: get to a clear vision, **prioritize use cases** so the order is deliberate rather than accidental, put money and effort into capability, carry the organisation through the change, measure what it was worth, and keep responsible AI on the agenda.<sup>[2]</sup>

That list overlaps the one above, which is Google's doing rather than a transcription error, and the overlap is informative. Two items appear in both: measuring value, and responsible AI. If something turns up twice in a short published list, it is the part the publisher is worried you will skip.

**Change management** is the item in this list and not the other, and it is worth naming as a leader's responsibility rather than a project activity. A gen AI tool that nobody adopts has the same business impact as one that does not work.

## Every Role Has a Different Job in the Rollout

One question shape asks what a specific layer of the organisation should do, and the answer depends on the layer.

Mid-level managers are the case that gets tested. What they contribute, and nobody else can, is **identifying high-impact and feasible use cases inside the workflows they own**.<sup>[3]</sup><sup>[4]</sup> They sit where the two directions meet: close enough to the work to see the friction, senior enough to be heard. Options that have them writing the enterprise strategy, choosing the platform, or building the thing are all describing somebody else's job.

The general rule, which transfers to any role in a question like this: **the right answer is the thing that layer can see and others cannot.**

## Proving It Worked

Measurement is the second half of this objective and it has one reliable answer. The blueprint asks you to identify techniques to **measure impact**, and when a scenario wants the most direct way to do it, the answer is **the operational indicators that function already uses**.<sup>[1]</sup> Note what is not on Google's list of techniques: a single headline **ROI** figure. Return on investment is what a programme is eventually judged on, and it is assembled from operational numbers rather than measured directly.

For a support organisation that means things like how long a case takes to handle and whether it is resolved on first contact. Those are the numbers that were on the wall before any of this started, which is exactly what makes them persuasive.

What loses, every time:

| The tempting measure | Why it fails as business impact |
|---|---|
| Model accuracy | An internal property of the model, not an outcome for anyone |
| Usage counts, logins, prompts sent | Measures adoption, not benefit, and rises when a tool is confusing |
| User satisfaction surveys about the tool | Measures feelings about the tool, not the work |
| Volume of output produced | More output is not better work, and can be worse |

**A measure that only the project team cares about is not evidence of impact.** *It is evidence of a project.* The test is whether somebody outside the initiative was already tracking the number, because a number they were already tracking is a number they already believe.

## How Wrong Answers Are Built on This Material

This objective has the least documentation behind it of anything on the exam, which means its questions test Google's published position on strategy. The distractors are all sensible management, pointed slightly wrong.

- **The half-of-Google's-answer trap.** Top-down alone, or bottom-up alone, where the recommendation combines them. The most reliable wrong answer on this objective because it is correct as far as it goes.
- **The wrong-layer answer.** A duty belonging to another level of the organisation: executives writing strategy, engineers choosing tools, managers doing either.
- **The internal-metric answer.** Model accuracy, or usage, where business impact was asked for.
- **The technology-as-strategy answer.** Adopting a platform, or standardising on a model, offered as a strategy. Both are decisions that follow a strategy.
- **The one-big-launch answer.** A single large deployment where the recommendation is focused implementations plus continuing exploration and refinement.

| When the scenario | The answer is usually | The trap is usually |
|---|---|---|
| Asks which strategy Google recommends | The one combining top-down and bottom-up | Top-down alone, accurately described |
| Asks what mid-level managers contribute | Finding high-impact, feasible cases in their own workflows | Writing strategy, or choosing the platform |
| Asks how to measure business impact | That function's existing operational KPIs | Model accuracy, or usage counts |
| Describes a tool nobody uses | Change management and adoption | A technical improvement to the tool |
| Asks what a gen AI strategy must include | Focus, exploration, responsibility, resourcing, impact, improvement | One element presented as the whole |

The habit: for any strategy question, ask whether the option covers both directions and whether it names something the business already measures. Those two tests between them settle most of this objective.

## What to Remember

- **Two directions at once.** Google recommends **combining top-down**, where leadership sets vision and strategy, **with bottom-up**, where employees find the practical applications and give feedback.
- **Google names the halves and never names the combination**, so an option has to invent a word for it, often **multi-directional**. The option that **is not a quotation** is the one that is **not half an answer**.
- **Half of a two-sided recommendation is a wrong answer.** It is the most convincing distractor here because it is **true as far as it goes**.
- **Six strategy elements**: **strategic focus, exploration, responsible AI, resourcing, impact, continuous improvement**.
- **Focus and exploration pull against each other on purpose.** Holding both is **the job**, not a contradiction to resolve.
- **Six planning steps**: a **clear vision**, **use cases put in order**, **investment in capability**, **change carried through**, **value measured**, and **responsible AI championed**.
- **Measuring value and responsible AI appear in both lists.** Something listed twice is what Google **expects you to skip**.
- **Change management is a leader's responsibility.** A tool nobody adopts has **the same impact as one that does not work**.
- **Each layer answers for what it alone can see.** Mid-level managers contribute **high-impact, feasible use cases inside their own workflows**.
- **Business impact is measured in the function's existing operational KPIs**, such as **handling time and first-contact resolution**.
- **Model accuracy, usage counts and output volume are not business impact.** The test: **was anybody outside the project already tracking this number?**
